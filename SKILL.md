# SEO Blog Strategy — Ahrefs and MicroConf

Audit and structure blog content for relevant organic discovery and AI-search visibility. Default to the founder's actual app, audience, and business model. Help readers solve a real problem; keep recommendations grounded in inspected content, product facts, and available research.

## Sources and references

The combined source is [references/seo-aeo-playbook.md](references/seo-aeo-playbook.md), based on the Ahrefs course and MicroConf lightning talk linked inside it. It includes editorial applications and dated October 2026 technical checks; these are not all claims made by both speakers.

Read selectively:

- **Audit:** Steps 3, 5–8, and 11–13 of the playbook as relevant, plus [references/audit-and-brief.md](references/audit-and-brief.md) for evaluation criteria and output fields.
- **New post or blog:** Steps 2–7 and the content brief, plus the new-content structures in [references/audit-and-brief.md](references/audit-and-brief.md).
- **Visibility gaps, off-site work, videos, or measurement:** Steps 1–4, 9–10, and 13–14 when requested or needed for the decision.

Do not load all references or run a full technical/competitive audit for a narrow editorial request. The skill is self-contained and does not require the original chat, transcripts, an Ahrefs subscription, or another skill.

## 1. Identify the unit of work

Use existing context, supplied research, product documentation, and product-marketing notes before asking questions. Select the appropriate route:

- **Existing post:** Audit the actual article and its relevant next-step links.
- **Existing blog:** Inspect the content inventory and a relevant set of posts; assess topic coverage, overlap, priorities, and shared patterns.
- **New post:** Establish the audience, topic, intent, and evidence; produce a useful brief and outline.
- **New blog:** Establish the audience and product; organize distinct topic families, initial post opportunities, and internal relationships.

If the user says "blog" ambiguously, infer the unit from the supplied URL or context. Ask only if the distinction materially changes the work. A new post does not require a sitewide strategy first.

Preserve the actual conversion: for prelaunch apps, launch notifications; for live software, its real self-serve access or purchase flow. Do not introduce consultation funnels or treat a waitlist entry as a sale. Do not assume a trial, free plan, feature, integration, or release date exists.

## 2. Inspect before judging

- For a URL, read the actual page. Inspect the rendered layout, headings, and destinations when needed and tools permit. Use accessible archives, sitemaps, or supplied inventories to scope a blog-level review.
- For local content, locate the relevant articles, metadata, routes, and link destinations. Distinguish source-code observations from a live page inspection.
- For pasted text, assess the text; do not infer speed, indexability, structured data, rendered headings, or working links from prose alone.
- If a source cannot be accessed, disclose that and work with accessible material. Do not claim to have reviewed the page.

Record inspected URLs/files and coverage limits. Reviewing an index or a sample does not establish that every post was audited. Keep observations from analytics exports separate from editorial judgments.

Maintain a compact evidence brief: intended reader; actual product and status; business goal; topic and current alternatives; source traffic or target query family; observed intent and format; product connection; available examples/data; constraints and unknowns. Mark important inputs as **observed**, **founder-confirmed**, **customer evidence**, **hypothesis**, or **unknown**.

## 3. Research the question, not just a keyword

Use the playbook's BID checks: **business potential**, **intent**, and **difficulty**. Inspect relevant search results when making claims about current intent, competitors, or ranking formats. Retain the search context, date, and evidence behind those judgments.

Use Ahrefs or user-supplied exports if available. Without them, use actual public results, product facts, and user research; explicitly leave volume, difficulty metrics, traffic estimates, and AI share of voice unverified. Never fabricate metrics or present generated keyword suggestions as measured demand.

Consider whether an AI answer may satisfy the question without a visit, and whether a citation or brand recommendation would still have business value. Use prompt variations and fan-out topics to discover missing coverage, not to generate near-duplicate pages for every wording.

When missing information changes the direction, ask a few focused questions, usually 3–5 or fewer. For a blank slate, establish the app, intended reader, and problem first. Use existing answers rather than restarting discovery. Where research is unavailable, provide a provisional brief and label the assumptions instead of requiring paid tools or indefinite interviews.

## 4. Audit existing content

Apply the reference's rubric using **supported / gap / unknown / not applicable**. Prioritize factual errors and broken experiences, then intent and business relevance, then substance, structure, evidence, freshness, product clarity, and experience. Preserve useful sections and existing evidence.

For each consequential finding, provide the observed wording or section, why it matters, a specific proposed correction, and the evidence or uncertainty. Separate factual corrections from editorial hypotheses and measured performance problems. Do not invent aggregate SEO scores, universal benchmarks, predicted citation gains, or guaranteed rankings.

For a blog-level audit, additionally inventory the inspected content, identify genuine topic gaps and overlapping purposes, and classify work as **fix / build / influence**. Prioritize updates by business relevance and available evidence. Similar keywords alone do not prove cannibalization; recommend consolidation or redirects only with a supported reason and preserve useful destinations.

Deliver the audit, prioritized changes, unknowns, and a proportionate validation plan. An audit-only request does not automatically become a full rewrite. If revisions are requested, proceed once the important inputs suffice; implement only the authorized content or code changes.

## 5. Structure a new post or blog

For a **new post**, deliver a grounded brief and section-by-section outline using the reference. Include:

- Intended reader, task, query family, intent evidence, and product/business connection.
- Recommended format and a proposed title/H1, slug, and search-description draft appropriate to the publishing context.
- An answer-first opening or sample opening, with unsupported claims excluded.
- Descriptive H2/H3s with the answer or purpose of each section, needed examples, evidence, and genuine questions to resolve.
- Useful tables, steps, demonstrations, or comparisons where the subject calls for them.
- Existing internal links where inspected, and clearly marked proposed destinations where pages do not exist yet.
- The actual relevant product action, research gaps, and how to judge the result.

Choose format from the user's need: tutorial, integration guide, review, comparison, shortlist, original research, or a utility/template. A comparison must explain criteria and trade-offs. Do not imply hands-on testing or independent endorsement without evidence.

For a **new blog**, organize a manageable, prioritized topic map around distinct reader problems and business-relevant use cases. Identify each proposed page's intent, format, purpose, relationship to existing/product pages, and research status. Separate confirmed demand from ideas. Expand priority topics into briefs when requested or useful; do not automatically write a large batch of articles.

If full drafting is requested, turn the agreed or sufficiently grounded structure into complete copy without adding an unnecessary approval step. If not, stop at the usable brief or content architecture. Do not publish articles, change live CMS settings, contact publishers, or buy tools merely because the plan recommends those actions.

## 6. Apply the writing and evidence rules

- Put the answer first, then explain conditions, examples, and evidence. Avoid delaying the point with generic background.
- Make headings reflect actual questions. Keep major sections understandable independently without forced sentence-by-sentence brand repetition or artificial tiny chunks.
- Name brands, products, features, and their real relationships explicitly. Brand an original concept only when the attribution is accurate; naming is not evidence of superiority or guaranteed attribution.
- Prefer clear declarative language, real examples, and original information over filler or paraphrased search results.
- Use ordered steps, bullets, and tables where they clarify the subject. Cite primary evidence for consequential factual claims and retain its context.
- Match length to the question. The course's under-1,000-word observation is not a length cap; listicles, schema, and fresh timestamps are not automatic citation tactics.
- Refresh meaningful facts, workflows, screenshots, and sources. Diagnose declines before assuming staleness; changing a date alone is not an update.
- Keep product claims, pricing, comparisons, planned capabilities, testimonials, and data accurate. Missing proof remains a research task outside publishable copy.

## 7. Handle technical and performance claims carefully

The playbook is a dated source snapshot. Before recommending consequential changes involving crawlers, robots.txt, structured data, rich-result eligibility, rendering, analytics reports, or provider behavior, verify current official documentation for the relevant system. Clearly distinguish an updated technical fact from the source talks' historical advice. If verification is unavailable, mark the claim unverified and avoid categorical prescriptions.

Keep search access and training choices distinct; do not advise allowing all bots as an SEO prerequisite. Check access and rendering evidence rather than assuming a framework is invisible. Use relevant markup matching visible content and actual current eligibility; never promise citations or rich results from FAQ/HowTo examples or llms.txt.

Do not infer a citation from a bot request, attribute all direct visits to AI, equate tool estimates with observed impressions, or treat one generated answer as a stable ranking. Distinguish mentions, links, recommendations, visits, signups, meaningful use, and payments.

When results are available, compare consistent query sets, platforms, dates, cohorts, and observation windows. Correlation and an improvement after an update do not by themselves establish causation. Allow inconclusive results with low traffic.

## 8. Review the deliverable

Check that the proposed structure answers the actual question, serves the intended reader, provides a credible product connection, preserves useful nuance, and identifies its evidence gaps. Clearly label the review as editorial, technical, or data-informed according to what was actually inspected.

For edits, summarize what changed, why, what was checked, and material limitations. For briefs, distinguish confirmed facts from proposed research and future assets. Avoid expanding a focused post request into every topic covered by the playbook.

## Example requests

- "Audit this existing blog post for SEO and AI-search visibility: [URL]."
- "Review my blog and tell me which posts to update and which relevant topics are missing."
- "Give me a brief and outline for a new comparison article about my app."
- "My app has no blog yet. Help me organize the first useful topic clusters."
