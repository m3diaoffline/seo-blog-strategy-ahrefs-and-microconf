# Blog audit and content-brief reference

These are editorial operating templates derived from the combined playbook. They are not measured ranking factors or additional claims from the source speakers. Adapt the fields to the request.

## Existing-content rubric

Assess relevant items as **supported**, **gap**, **unknown**, or **not applicable**. A page saying something is not independent proof that the claim is true. Keep actual performance observations separate from judgment of the writing.

| Criterion | What to examine | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Reader and task | Is the intended reader and problem specific? | Actual copy, product context, user research. |
| Business relevance | Could this topic bring appropriate discovery or product use? | Plausible relationship to the product; conversion data when available. |
| Intent and format | Does the page answer the task and match the relevant decision? | Observed results and reader questions; mark current results unknown if not inspected. |
| Answer-first opening | Can readers quickly find the actual answer and conditions? | The opening and section lead-ins. |
| Useful substance | Does it provide explanations, examples, evidence, or demonstrations beyond generic summaries? | Actual examples, screenshots, methods, and primary sources. |
| Heading structure | Are headings descriptive, ordered, and answered by their sections? | Rendered headings or verified source markup. Plain text alone may not establish HTML levels. |
| Independent sections | Can a substantial section be understood without missing context? | Section wording, named subject, qualifications. |
| Entities and brand | Are product names, capabilities, and relationships explicit and accurate? | Product facts and observed text. |
| Evidence | Do links and demonstrations support the associated claims? | Original sources, real data with methods, honest product demonstrations. |
| Freshness | Are relevant facts and instructions current? | Dates of underlying facts, product changes, current authoritative sources. |
| Presentation | Do lists, steps, tables, and visuals clarify the subject? | Inspected content and layout; distinguish visible design from text-only review. |
| Links and next action | Are relevant destinations correct, useful, and consistent with availability? | Inspected links and confirmed journey, without transacting. |
| Access and indexing | Can intended public content be fetched and discovered? | HTTP behavior, indexing tools, robots directives, initial and rendered content where checked. |
| Markup and metadata | Are titles, descriptions, and any structured data accurate and relevant? | Actual source, validation, and current official eligibility requirements. |
| Measurement | Are traffic and business outcomes distinguished from citation or mention estimates? | Dated analytics, query set, source, cohort, and observation window. |

Do not apply numerical weights to this table or convert it into a predictive score. A missing optional video, comparison table, FAQ, schema type, or keyword count is not automatically a problem.

## Audit delivery

Begin with the most consequential conclusion and the inspected scope. Preserve what is effective. For each important finding, use enough of this structure to make it reviewable:

```text
Page / inspected section:
Observation or short exact excerpt:
Assessment: supported / gap / unknown / not applicable
Why it matters to this reader or business goal:
Proposed fix:
Basis: factual correction / editorial hypothesis / measured issue
Evidence limits or needed research:
Priority and proportionate validation:
```

For a blog-level review, add a content inventory: inspected URL, topic, reader task, observed format, available performance evidence, proposed action, and reason. Identify the subset actually inspected. Group recurring issues instead of repeating the same advice for every post.

```text

H2: [descriptive heading]
  Direct answer or purpose:
  H3 subsections, only where useful:
  Evidence / concrete example / demonstration:
  Relevant limitations:

[Repeat for necessary sections; do not force a fixed count.]

Useful list, steps, or comparison table, if warranted:
Actual reader questions / FAQ, if warranted:
Existing internal links inspected:
Proposed pages or assets, clearly labeled:
Primary external evidence needed:

Confirmed inputs:
Assumptions / unanswered research:

```

Do not fill an evidence field with invented data. A proposed screenshot, benchmark, comparison test, or internal page is a production task until it actually exists.

## New-blog topic map

Use a compact table or list with these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Topic family and specific page idea | Keep each page's intended purpose distinct. |
| Intended reader/task | Explain why someone needs the page. |
| Format and intent | Explain why a tutorial, comparison, review, or other format fits. |
| Product connection | Identify an accurate connection to a real capability or user problem. |
| Existing/proposed destination | Avoid presenting a planned page as an existing asset. |
| Relationships | Identify useful links to product pages and related articles. |
| Evidence and research status | Distinguish observed demand and results from hypotheses. |
| Priority and next action | Explain what to research, improve, or create first. |

Keep the initial plan manageable. Do not invent search volume, prescribe publishing frequency without considering capacity, or equate the number of posts with topical authority. Useful coverage and maintained accuracy matter more than a quota.
