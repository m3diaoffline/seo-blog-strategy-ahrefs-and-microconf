# SEO Playbook October 2026 — Ahrefs and MicroConf

## One workflow for organic search and AI-search visibility

**Edition:** October 2026. Prepared from both supplied transcripts and the user's takeaway notes, with overlapping lessons merged. Technical documentation checked on October 19, 2026.

**Primary sources**

1. [Ahrefs AEO course — Sam Oh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uza9GX0E2mw): the comprehensive course on how AI search works, research, content, mentions, YouTube, technical access, and measurement.
2. [MicroConf lightning talk — Jesse of DropInBlog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nyq38GvRm44): practical on-page improvements, examples, and repurposing important articles into videos.

The supplied transcripts are the basis of this synthesis. The linked videos provide attribution; no missing slides, downloadable templates, or demonstrations are assumed to have been reviewed. Worksheets and software examples below are editorial applications. Clearly labeled **October 2026 technical notes** update or qualify advice using linked official documentation.

**Objective:** Help the right people discover your product through search results, AI recommendations, useful content, and third-party mentions—and connect that visibility to meaningful product outcomes.

**Core strategy:** Keep the SEO foundation. Build useful, accurate content that answers real questions, establish clear associations between your brand and its category, earn credible mentions, and measure both visibility and customer behavior.

---

## Step 1 — Understand what you are trying to influence

### Traditional SEO and AEO work together

- **SEO:** Make pages discoverable and useful enough to appear in organic search results and attract relevant visits.
- **AEO:** Make your brand and content useful sources for systems that generate answers. GEO and LLMO are overlapping terms used for similar work.

The courses describe two sources behind AI answers: information learned during training and information retrieved at answer time. Your practical opportunities are to make relevant public information accessible and earn credible coverage. Publishing something does not guarantee inclusion in training, retrieval, or a future answer.

### Query fan-out changes topic research

An AI system may expand one question into several related searches. A question about software for a small business could lead to searches about price, features, alternatives, use cases, and integrations.

Use those related questions to identify missing topic coverage. Do not create a separate page for every synthetic query: fan-out varies, and many generated queries have no measurable human search volume.

### Track more than links

| Outcome | Meaning | Practical value |
|---|---|---|
| Brand mentioned | The answer names your brand. | Awareness and possible later branded searches. |
| Your page cited | The answer links to your site as a source. | An opportunity for referral visits. The answer may cite a page without recommending the product. |
| Product recommended | The answer presents your product as a suitable option. | Relevant discovery, with or without a link. |
| Absent or inaccurate | Your brand is missing or described incorrectly. | A gap to investigate. |

AI outputs vary across runs, topics, and platforms. Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, and Perplexity do not necessarily cite the same sources. Audit the platforms relevant to your audience rather than assuming one result represents all AI search.

**Decision:** For each topic, identify whether you primarily want a visit, a recommendation, or accurate brand information. Those outcomes require different measurement.

## Step 2 — Establish your brand and visibility baseline

### Map your entities

List the main brand, app names, meaningful feature names, original frameworks, and relevant founder or expert names. Connect each to the problems, users, and capabilities it should be associated with.

```text
Entity / exact name:
Category and intended user:
Problem or task it addresses:
Actual capability or distinctive approach:
Official page supporting that description:
Known naming variations:
```

Keep intended positioning separate from what you can substantiate. A desired association is not evidence that the product already delivers it.

### Record the starting position

Use existing search and analytics data, plus Ahrefs where available:

- **Site Explorer:** Organic keywords, traffic, important pages, backlinks, and referring domains.
- **Brand Radar:** Brand mentions, cited pages and domains, competitor presence, topics, and estimated visibility by platform.
- **Search Console and site analytics:** Actual search performance, visits, and completed actions.

Save the date, geography or language where relevant, platform, query set, and filters. Third-party impressions and share-of-voice figures describe a tool's dataset; they are not a complete census of AI conversations.

### Organize findings into six gaps

| Gap | Question |
|---|---|
| Visibility | Where do competitors appear while we do not? |
| Narrative | Is the brand described accurately and in its proper category? |
| Topic | Which relevant problems or use cases are not associated with us? |
| Format | Are useful tutorials, comparisons, reviews, or videos missing? |
| Web mentions | Which relevant external pages include competitors but omit us? |
| Demand | Where is there relevant interest that our brand or content does not capture? |

Audit a small set of realistic questions as well as broader reports. Record whether the answer names you, links to you, recommends you, and describes you correctly. Repeat observations over time; a single generated answer is a weak baseline.

**Output:** A dated baseline and a gap list tied to actual topics and sources.

## Step 3 — Find topics with business value

### Start with seeds and modifiers

Build short seed terms for your category, user tasks, and problems. Expand them with modifiers that reveal intent and formats:

- Discovery and evaluation: **best, top, review, versus, comparison, alternative**.
- Education: **how to, what is, guide, tutorial**.
- Action and utility: **calculator, checker, generator, tool, template, finder, planner, maker**.

The Ahrefs workflow is Keywords Explorer → Matching terms → relevant include filters. An AI assistant can suggest seed ideas; use actual results and research to vet them.

### Apply BID

| Test | What to investigate |
|---|---|
| **Business potential** | Would reaching this searcher plausibly lead to relevant product discovery, use, or payment? |
| **Intent** | What is ranking, and does the format match what the searcher wants? |
| **Difficulty** | What quality, expertise, links, and resources would be needed to compete? |

Inspect the results themselves. Domain Rating and referring-domain counts are research aids, not guarantees or eligibility thresholds. Low-authority examples in the talks show opportunity, not that authority no longer matters.

### Add the AI-answer check

Ask: **Could the result page or an AI answer satisfy this need without a visit?**

- If yes, assess whether being mentioned or cited would still have value.
- If the person needs to use a product, inspect the actual utility or signup opportunity.
- If neither the click nor the likely brand exposure helps your business, deprioritize the topic despite attractive volume.

The Ahrefs course highlights tools as opportunities because people often need to perform a task. Treat this as a research direction, not a claim that tools are immune to AI substitution or that these queries never show AI answers.

### Research prompts as topic families

In Brand Radar, inspect competitor-only responses and queries containing evaluation modifiers. Review cited sources and fan-out topics where available.

Collect several natural questions expressing the same decision, including user type, constraints, and use case. Build coverage of the underlying decision rather than pages for minor prompt variations.

**Output:** A shortlist with business relevance, observed intent, likely format, competition, and the intended discovery outcome.

## Step 4 — Prioritize fix, build, or influence

Classify each opportunity:

- **Fix:** Improve an existing page, outdated information, or an access problem.
- **Build:** Create a missing useful page, demonstration, comparison, or video.
- **Influence:** Earn relevant external coverage or correct inaccurate descriptions.

Prioritize meaningful business relevance, a clear evidence-backed gap, and achievable effort. Begin with important pages and topics where the product genuinely belongs. The MicroConf talk emphasizes commercially relevant articles over generic informational traffic; Ahrefs adds the broader research and gap-analysis process.

```text
Topic / page / external source:
Relevant user and business outcome:
Observed gap and evidence:
Action: fix / build / influence
Proposed change:
Effort and owner:
What we will measure:
Review date:
```

Avoid publishing a large volume of near-identical pages merely to cover keyword variants. Each page should provide a distinct useful answer or experience.

## Step 5 — Use one clear on-page writing method

This combines Ahrefs' writing principles with MicroConf's quick on-page improvements.

### Answer first: BLUF

Start the article or section with the answer, recommendation, or definition. Add context, evidence, limitations, and examples afterward. Remove long introductions that postpone the point.

For comparisons, state who each option fits before explaining the detailed trade-offs. An honest conditional answer is better than an unjustified universal winner.

### Match headings to intent

Use headings that name the question or subject. Replace labels such as “Introduction” or “More information” with useful descriptions.

For an article about a product workflow, a heading such as “How to export a project from [App]” communicates more than “Next steps.” Use a logical H1 → H2 → H3 structure; headings should reflect the content beneath them.

### Make sections understandable on their own

Read each main section out of context. Does it identify the product or topic, answer its heading, and preserve relevant conditions? Clarify ambiguous references such as “this tool” where needed.

This is the useful interpretation of **atomic content**. Do not force every sentence to repeat the product name or split an explanation into tiny fragments.

**October 2026 technical note:** Google says no ideal page length or special chunking is required for its generative search features. Use standalone sections for clarity, not as a mandatory AI format. [Google's AI optimization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

### Name entities and relationships

Write who does what, for whom, and in which situation.

```text
Vague: This tool helps with video editing.

Specific structure:
[App name]'s [actual feature] helps [intended user]
[complete a specific task] by [real product behavior].
```

Only fill in real capabilities. The user's examples involving MicroFilms and Slidekick illustrate explicit naming; they are not evidence of any product's capabilities.

### Keep sentences simple and declarative

Prefer one main idea per sentence, concrete verbs, and terms the intended reader understands. Keep necessary nuance and conditions rather than simplifying a claim until it becomes inaccurate.

### Use the right structure

- Ordered lists for sequences.
- Bullets for parallel items or criteria.
- Tables for comparisons, attributes, or structured facts.
- Direct questions and answers where the reader has specific concerns.

Use structure because it improves the answer, not to turn every paragraph into a list.

### Support factual claims

Link to the original research, documentation, or primary evidence behind material claims. Explain what your own data measures and how it was obtained. A citation should substantiate the associated statement; adding an authoritative-looking link does not validate an unrelated claim.

MicroConf emphasizes trusted sources and structure; Ahrefs emphasizes usefulness, specificity, and original information. Combine them into a clear answer with something worth learning.

**Output:** A page that answers immediately, scans well, remains understandable in sections, and supports its claims.

## Step 6 — Choose a useful format and the necessary length

### Formats to investigate

| Format | Useful when the reader needs… | What makes it credible |
|---|---|---|
| Best/top list | A shortlist of suitable options. | Explicit selection criteria, actual differences, and suitable users for each option. |
| Comparison / versus | Help choosing between alternatives. | Current capabilities, price context where relevant, and honest trade-offs. |
| Review | An evaluation of a particular product. | A clear basis for the assessment; do not imply testing that never happened. |
| Tutorial / integration guide | Help completing a task. | Accurate steps, real examples, and current screenshots or demonstrations. |
| Original research | Evidence answering a question. | A method, source data, limitations, and specific findings. |
| Tool / template | Something to use or apply. | A functioning, relevant utility or a usable artifact. |

Both sources show that lists and comparisons can be useful citation sources. Choose them where the audience's decision calls for them. A vendor-written comparison is not independent third-party endorsement; keep its perspective clear.

### Correct interpretation of the 1,000-word takeaway

The Ahrefs transcript reports that 53.4% of analyzed cited pages were under 1,000 words and that word count had approximately a 0.04 correlation with citations. This is a description of that dataset, not a maximum length or proof that short pages cause citations.

Write enough to answer the question well. Remove filler, preserve useful evidence, and let the subject determine length.

## Step 7 — Strengthen brand associations

Make it easy to connect your brand to its real products, capabilities, and ideas.

1. Use the same meaningful product and feature names across the site and public materials.
2. Attach the brand to original frameworks or proprietary concepts when that attribution is accurate.
3. Define the concept plainly and show it in use.
4. Explain the same relationship consistently in articles, documentation, video descriptions, and other relevant channels.
5. Publish accurate official answers about the product so important facts are not left ambiguous.

For example, “[Brand]'s [feature]” is useful when discussing that product's implementation. There is no need to rename every generic category or make ordinary terminology sound proprietary.

Ahrefs discusses retaining attribution for original ideas; MicroConf illustrates naming and publishing a framework. Neither implies that branding a concept guarantees credit in future AI answers.

## Step 8 — Refresh valuable pages with real improvements

Start with important pages that are stale or declining, especially where useful authority or links already exist.

### Workflow

1. In Site Explorer's Top pages report, inspect traffic declines and existing referring domains.
2. Check the actual page and search results before diagnosing the cause. A decline can reflect changed intent, technical issues, lost links, competition, or outdated content.
3. Update facts, screenshots, product details, examples, comparisons, broken links, and source material as needed.
4. Apply Step 5 where the structure is weak.
5. Record the substantive changes and a truthful update date.
6. Monitor results after recrawling and sufficient observation time.

Freshness matters when the underlying information changes. Changing only the date is not an update. An evergreen answer does not need arbitrary monthly rewriting, and a page with few backlinks can still need an accuracy correction.

**Output:** An update log that explains what changed and why, rather than a list of refreshed timestamps.

## Step 9 — Earn mentions where the topic is already discussed

Ahrefs' studies associate branded mentions with AI visibility. These are observational relationships, not proof that adding a certain number of mentions will cause an uplift. Relevance, source quality, and genuine credibility matter.

### Prioritize three source types

| Source | Practical approach |
|---|---|
| Independent editorial coverage | Identify relevant reviews, comparisons, niche publications, and creator videos where the product is genuinely useful. |
| Communities and user-generated content | Contribute helpful answers to relevant discussions, with a transparent relationship to the product. |
| Your own public properties | Explain the product consistently across the website, YouTube, and other useful public channels. Owned coverage is not independent validation. |

### Find specific opportunities

1. In Brand Radar, inspect cited domains and pages for the relevant topic and platform.
2. Find sources that name competitors but omit your product.
3. In Content Explorer, search relevant best/top/comparison content, exclude your brand, and inspect the page's topical relevance and referring domains.
4. For communities, inspect specific discussions already cited or ranking for relevant questions. The course also suggests researching a community domain's organic keywords in Site Explorer.
5. Shortlist targets based on genuine fit and the value you could add.

### Make the contribution useful

Provide evidence, accurate product information, a demonstration, original research, or relevant experience. Do not manufacture consensus through fake reviews, undisclosed endorsements, or repetitive promotional replies.

Monitor whether coverage disappears or becomes inaccurate. Correct your official material first if needed, then request correction of third-party errors with supporting evidence. Publishing a correction does not guarantee that every system immediately adopts it.

**Output:** A prioritized mention-opportunity list with a specific source, relevant topic, reason for inclusion, and proposed contribution.

## Step 10 — Turn important topics into useful YouTube videos

Both sources highlight YouTube. Ahrefs provides the research and publishing workflow; MicroConf demonstrates repurposing commercially relevant articles and integration tutorials into videos.

### Find search opportunities

- In Site Explorer, research YouTube watch pages and their organic keywords. Inspect videos ranking near the top for your niche.
- In Brand Radar, inspect relevant topics and filter for YouTube citations or mentioned domains where the report supports it.
- Study the actual results: do people need a tutorial, comparison, demonstration, or shortlist?

Prioritize repeatable search demand and product-relevant problems rather than chasing views unrelated to the business. Tool-reported visibility is an opportunity signal, not proof that a topic will perform for your channel.

### Publish with this checklist

1. **Title:** Include the main topic or keyword naturally and make the subject unmistakable.
2. **Thumbnail:** Communicate why someone should click, while accurately representing the video.
3. **Description:** Write a genuine summary. Include the main topic early and link to the relevant supporting page or product destination.
4. **Timestamps:** Add accurate chapters with descriptive names so individual sections are easy to find.
5. **Spoken explanation:** Actually explain the subject in the video. Do not rely on a title containing words absent from the content.
6. **Format:** Match the audience's task and the successful formats you observed, while contributing your own useful demonstration or perspective.
7. **Transcript:** Check available captions or transcript for important product names and technical terms. This is an editorial implementation check, not a guaranteed ranking factor.

An important article can become a video addressing the same need, with links between the two where useful. Show the actual workflow rather than reading a thin article over generic footage.

A published video can be discovered or cited, but its existence is not proof that a provider trained on it or will recommend it.

**Output:** A useful video and supporting article serving the same product-relevant topic.

## Step 11 — Make public content accessible and technically sound

### Check discovery, access, and indexing

1. Confirm important public pages return the intended status and can be reached through normal links.
2. Inspect robots.txt, indexing directives, authentication, CDN rules, and bot challenges for unintended restrictions.
3. Ensure key content is available in accessible HTML, with sensible headings and descriptive links.
4. Compare the initial HTML with the rendered page where content depends heavily on JavaScript. Consider server rendering or pre-rendering if essential public content is otherwise absent from relevant fetches.
5. Use performance tests and actual requests to investigate slow or failed responses.

Crawler rendering differs. Disabling JavaScript is a useful diagnostic, not proof of how every assistant accesses the page. Verify the behavior relevant to your site instead of assuming that a framework is automatically invisible.

### Separate search access from training choices

**October 2026 technical note:** OpenAI documents independent controls: `OAI-SearchBot` supports ChatGPT search; `GPTBot` concerns potential training use. `ChatGPT-User` handles certain user-triggered visits and is not the automatic search crawler. Blocking training is not the same decision as blocking search. [OpenAI crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).

**October 2026 technical note:** Google says `Google-Extended` controls specified Gemini training and grounding uses; it does not control inclusion or ranking in Google Search. It is a robots control token, not a separate HTTP user-agent. Review Search access through the appropriate Googlebot and indexing controls. [Google crawler documentation](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers).

Inspect what your hosting and security configuration actually does; do not assume a vendor's default behavior. Choose access settings deliberately rather than removing every restriction to pursue citations.

### Recover useful visits landing on missing URLs

Inspect analytics and server responses for repeatedly requested URLs that return 404s, including AI-referred visits. Fix broken internal links. Redirect an incorrect or retired URL only when a relevant destination exists; do not send every missing URL to the homepage. Keep a helpful 404 experience for genuinely missing content.

## Step 12 — Use structured data appropriately

The MicroConf talk emphasizes schema, including FAQ and HowTo examples. The Ahrefs course is more cautious about evidence for a direct AI-citation benefit. The practical synthesis is to make the visible content excellent, then add accurate markup where it has a legitimate purpose.

1. Select a schema type that describes the page's real content.
2. Make the markup consistent with visible, current information.
3. Validate it using the appropriate tools and check the target feature's current requirements.
4. Do not mark up invented reviews, unsupported ratings, or material the page does not provide.

**October 2026 technical note:** Google does not require special schema for generative search. It also says llms.txt does not improve or harm Google Search visibility. Neither should displace useful content or basic access work. Other services may have their own requirements. [Google's AI optimization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

**October 2026 technical note:** Google stopped showing HowTo rich results in 2023. Its documentation records FAQ rich results as discontinued from May 7, 2026. Keep useful instructions and Q&A, but do not promise Google rich results from those historical recommendations. [HowTo change](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes), [FAQ update](https://developers.google.com/search/updates#may-2026).

## Step 13 — Measure visibility and business outcomes together

Use complementary evidence. No single report captures the entire discovery journey.

### A. Search and AI-answer visibility

Track relevant queries and pages in Search Console and monitor brand mentions, citations, topics, and share of voice in your chosen research dataset. Preserve the platform, query set, date, and filters when comparing results.

**October 2026 technical note:** Google has introduced dedicated generative-AI performance reports in Search Console, with worldwide rollout announced as completed by August 31, 2026. Use those views for Google's AI features alongside overall Search performance; the course's measurement limitations should not be read as saying these reports cannot exist. [Google's report announcement](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports).

Check whether mentions are accurate, not just frequent. Keep distinct records of links to your site, external pages discussing your brand, and recommendations in generated answers.

### B. Human referral visits

Create an AI-referral channel or segment in your analytics using source values you actually observe. The course describes custom channel groups in GA4 and built-in grouping in Ahrefs Web Analytics.

Inspect landing pages, completed actions, and traffic quality. Referrer information may be absent or vary by app, platform, and link type; visible referral traffic can undercount influence. Do not automatically reclassify direct traffic as AI traffic.

### C. Bot requests

Use server logs or a bot-analytics integration to see which verified crawlers can fetch which pages, their response codes, and possible access problems. Distinguish automated crawls from user-triggered visits and human referrals.

**A bot hit is not evidence of a citation, recommendation, or customer.** It is an access or discovery signal to investigate alongside actual answer observations.

### D. Self-reported discovery

Ask “How did you hear about us?” at an appropriate point in signup, onboarding, or a customer survey. Include an AI-related option and allow details if useful. Avoid adding unnecessary friction to a critical conversion step.

Treat the answer as self-reported influence. It can reveal discovery missing from referral data, but it does not establish precise causal attribution. The same person may appear in multiple measurement sources, so do not simply add all counts together.

### Connect discovery to the actual product journey

| Stage | Useful outcome | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Prelaunch | Successful, non-duplicate waitlist entries. | Willingness to pay or product retention. |
| Available web app | Completed accounts, meaningful product use, paid subscriptions. | An account alone does not prove value or payment. |
| Existing subscribers | Continued use, renewals, and observed revenue over a consistent window. | A short sample does not establish lifetime value. |
| Mobile landing page | Store visits, with installs or subscriptions tracked separately where attribution supports it. | A store-link click is not an install. |

These product examples apply the courses' business-value principle to app founders. They are not claims about either speaker's specific conversion model.

### Evaluate progress

Compare relevant visibility, customer outcomes, and the time or money invested. Stronger results after a change support further investigation, but seasonality, product changes, competition, and platform changes can also affect them.

The talks' conversion multiples, one-week improvements, and platform citation percentages describe particular observations. Do not use them as forecasts for your site. Their practical lesson is to evaluate qualified discovery and downstream value rather than raw pageviews alone.

## Step 14 — Run a repeatable operating cycle

The following sequence consolidates both sources. The timing is an editorial working cadence, not a promised results schedule.

### First working week

1. Establish the entity map and a dated visibility baseline using Steps 1–2.
2. Check access and measurement so future work can be observed: Steps 11–13.
3. Select five to ten important existing pages, or a smaller realistic set, and diagnose their gaps.
4. Improve the highest-value pages using the single writing method in Step 5 and refresh process in Step 8.
5. Identify the strongest missing topics and ten plausible external mention opportunities, reducing the list if relevance or capacity is limited.

### Next publishing cycle

Choose the most useful next asset: an improved page, a missing comparison, an original demonstration, a functioning utility, or a video for a proven relevant topic. Record the hypothesis, publish an accurate asset, and make a plan to review it after discovery and enough observation time.

### Ongoing reviews

- **Weekly:** Review completed work, access failures, meaningful new referrals, and important factual errors; maintain a manageable backlog.
- **Monthly:** Compare brand visibility, topic coverage, mention accuracy, useful visits, and product outcomes against the saved baseline.
- **Quarterly:** Repeat the competitive gap analysis, revisit target topics and sources, and reassess whether the effort is producing business value.

Prioritize the next fix, build, or influence action from the evidence. Maintain useful assets rather than treating citation visibility as a one-time setup.

## Editorial check before publishing

- [ ] The topic serves a relevant user and has a plausible business purpose.
- [ ] The answer appears early and the headings describe the actual content.
- [ ] Sections remain understandable when read independently, without forced repetition.
- [ ] Product names, capabilities, comparisons, and dates are accurate.
- [ ] Claims have relevant evidence; examples and prototypes are identified honestly.
- [ ] Lists and tables improve comprehension where used.
- [ ] The format and length fit the question rather than an arbitrary quota.
- [ ] Public content is accessible and markup reflects what is visible.
- [ ] The next product action matches availability and actual terms.
- [ ] Measurement distinguishes mentions, citations, visits, product actions, and revenue.
